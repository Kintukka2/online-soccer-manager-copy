Visual System

Navy is theirs.
Chalk is ours.

The visual system for OSMWiz, a companion to Online Soccer Manager 26/27. Everything that shows the game's data was measured: pixel-sampled from the official store art at native resolution, and read from the live web client's computed styles and public stylesheet. Gamebasics publishes no style guide, so no value here was chosen by taste. The identity, and the chalk it is drawn in, are ours.

Identity

The name splits in two, and so does the design

OSMWIZ

OSMWiz is the working name. It follows the <game>WIZ pattern of FHCWiz, so the two read as one family. The name splits in two and so does the design:

  • OSM is the game's world. Anything that shows the game's own data (cards, ratings, squads, fixtures, tactics, money) is drawn in the measured system in Foundation through Components.
  • WIZ is ours. Anything that is our analysis rather than their data (this mark, counter-tactic reasoning, editorial diagrams, teaching material) is drawn in chalk.

One rule settles which: their data wears navy and gold, our thinking wears chalk.

The mark

A W drawn as a move on a tactics board. An O where it starts, two passes already played (solid), a run still to happen (dashed), and an orange arrowhead where it ends. It shares the chalk method with FHCWiz but tells a football story: pass, pass, run. It borrows nothing from the game, so it survives intact if the acronym ever has to go.

The wordmark

OSMWIZ in Anton, one line, with an orange rule beneath the full width. OSM sits in #899AA7; WIZ sits in #FFFFFF. The acronym names the subject and the half that is lit is ours. That is the trademark position made visible, and it is why Limits permits the acronym at all.

  • Never set OSM alone, in any face, at any size.
  • Never set OSMWIZ in one flat colour; the halves always differ in value.
  • The orange rule belongs to the wordmark. It is not a divider to reuse.
  • Never set the wordmark in Saira Condensed. Saira is the game's typeface; Anton is the family's.

Naming risk. "OSM" is also the universal shorthand for OpenStreetMap, and most store results for "OSM" are map apps. Store listings and page titles should always carry "Online Soccer Manager" in full beside OSMWiz.

Foundation

The ground everything sits on

OSM's dark is navy, never black. Every ground measured on every screen carries a blue hue between 205° and 215° at high saturation. Where FHC uses texture to stop black looking flat, OSM uses hue, and its surfaces are smooth. There is no grain anywhere in the game.

Backdrop

Panels never sit on navy. They sit on royal, a saturated blue measured at #022F6D on Upgrade and #00165D on Strategy. It carries faint marble veins and floodlight glow, or a stadium photo behind the line-up. The game's grammar is navy panels on royal ground; this page follows it by staging every component on royal.

Elevation, the OSM way

Light comes from the top. A raised panel has a lighter rim along its top edge (measured #69B1C5 on tactic cards and #0374B7 around the match panel). A raised button has a white gloss band over its top half and a dark bevel along its bottom. Nothing is lit from below, and there is no drop-shadow ladder.

Data goes light

The one place OSM leaves the dark: the squad table is white paper. Rows are #FFFFFF, the star player's row is #FFF8E5, and the section header is sky blue #0096E3. Dense data reads as a printed team sheet laid on the navy chrome, which is the opposite of FHC's dark standings list.

What changed in 26/27. The previous store art (OSM-170/191, still served in the AR, ES and MX storefronts) drew line-ups as small blue mini-cards with fitness and morale bars. 26/27 replaced them with gold-framed landscape cards and moved fitness into a toggle. Build to 26/27.

Type

Saira Condensed for weight, the system grotesk for everything else

Unlike FHC's, OSM's typeface is identifiable. The live web client loads Saira Condensed at 400, 500, 700 and 800 from Google Fonts, with Sofia Sans Condensed as its fallback Verified. The app sets names and numbers in the same face at 800. It sets UI labels and table data in the platform's system grotesk: Helvetica Neue on iOS, Roboto on Android Measured. Both are open, so the companion can use the game's actual type.

Texture

Five layers, zero image files

OSM is glossy and smooth, with no grain and no noise. Its texture is light: gloss, metal, marble, floodlight. All five layers below are CSS gradients and one inline SVG, about 2KB in total.

1 · Gloss band  ·  bevel

Lifted verbatim from the web client's .btn-new. The bevel is an inset bottom shadow plus an inset top highlight: inset 0 -4px 0 -2px rgba(0,0,0,.4), inset 0 4px 1px -2px rgba(255,255,255,.4). The gloss is an ::after covering the top half (inset 4px top, 8px sides) at 40% white, with an inner glow in the button's own colour. Every raised control has both. See Components.

2 · Gold hairline

A 3px metal rule under the top bar. It is the most constant motif in the game, present on every screen with a top bar. The same metal frames every gold card.

3 · Marble veins

Thin white veins across the royal backdrop, and solid white marble on card corners. Fractal noise is thresholded into bands by one feComponentTransfer, so the veins come from a 600-byte data URI and no image.

4 · Floodlights

Two soft radial glows at the top corners over royal. They stand in for the game's stadium photography, which we do not use.

5 · Card streak

A diagonal amber light band across every default-theme card face. It marks a surface as a card at a glance.

Chalk

The WIZ half, and the only thing here not measured from the game

Everything above exists so the companion can show the game's data faithfully. Chalk exists so that when we explain something, such as why a counter works or what a formation leaves open, it is obvious who is talking.

The board

#0D1410Board ground
#2C3A30Board rim · 2 units
#E8F3EAChalk, primary
#9BB0A2Chalk, secondary
#FC8C21The orange pen · reused

A green-black slate on a navy page. It is the only green-tinted dark in the system, so a board can never be mistaken for the game's chrome. 2 units of rim and a 6-unit radius on a 100-unit width. No gloss, no marble, no metal, no hairline. A board is a figure, not a panel: it holds a drawing, and body copy goes outside it.

Why the board is not a pitch. OSM draws its own tactics on glossy 3D pitches with red tube arrows (Game plan) and on flat bright-green fields with blue arrows (Line tactics). Our diagrams must never look like those. A matte slate with chalk strokes cannot be confused with either.

The vocabulary: five marks and no more

Real coaching notation, so anyone who has stood at a whiteboard can read it. A sixth mark has to be added here first.

The arrowhead is the only orange on a board, and there is never more than one per figure. It is the conclusion. Two arrowheads mean the figure is saying two things and should be two figures.

Never on a cardCards, frames, numerals and rating circles are the game's objects.

One board per screenA second competes with the first and neither reads.

Construction

Inline SVG on a viewBox 100 units wide, height set by the figure. No image files, no icon font, no drawing library. That keeps a board crisp from a 16px favicon to a full-width diagram, and lets a share card for Discord or WhatsApp be rendered from the same source.

Rules

Seven decisions that settle most questions

Navy, never black

Every dark surface carries hue 205–215°. A neutral black anywhere outside a chalk board is a bug: it is the single fastest tell that a screen isn't OSM.

Light from the top

Raised means a lighter top rim, a gloss band on the upper half and a dark bevel beneath. Nothing is lit from below and nothing floats on a drop-shadow ladder.

Gold frames, never fills

Gold is a hairline, a frame, a numeral or VS. It is never a panel. The one exception is a gold card theme, which is the game's data, not our styling.

Green means growth

Neon greens fill what grows: training, fitness, division progress, a won match. A green button means gain, and nothing else is green.

Selection lights the label

Selected darkens the fill and turns the label #FFE800. Never a rim, never a glow, never a fill swap to another hue.

Data goes light

Dense rows are white paper with a sky header and a cream highlight. Chrome stays navy. Don't build a dark squad table: it is FHC's pattern, not OSM's.

Navy reports, chalk explains

A surface showing the game's numbers takes the measured system. A surface carrying our reasoning takes the board. Never mix them on one object.

Limits

What we deliberately don't take

OSMWiz is a companion tool, not a Gamebasics or Miniclip product. Mirroring the visual language is fair; taking the identity is not. Gamebasics' Terms (A.7) reserve its names, logos and designs, and the companion follows them.

  • Never the logo. No OSM wordmark, no white-outlined orange letterforms, no gold "26/27" numerals, no "25 Years" ribbon.
  • The acronym only inside our wordmark. OSM appears as the first half of OSMWiz and nowhere else. "Online Soccer Manager" is written out only to name the game in prose.
  • No official framing. No copy implying endorsement, partnership or affiliation with Gamebasics or Miniclip.
  • No photography and no crests. Player photos and club crests are licensed. Cards take a silhouette; crests take an empty disc.
  • No characters. The manager, commentator and coach mascots are the game's IP.
  • No lifted art. Marble, baroque gold flourishes, the hexagon mesh and stadium photos are approximated in CSS, never extracted. No assets from the app package. Decompiling it breaches Terms A.7.
  • No currency icons. The Boss Coin shield and the cash coin are drawn generically as "BC" and "$".
